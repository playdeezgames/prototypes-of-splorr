## Page text

**Latest version:** https://playdeezgames.github.io/prototypes-of-splorr/ (also add it as an external link on the page)

In August 1979, *SoftSide* magazine printed five BASIC programs for readers to type in by hand. These are new takes on all five, rebuilt for the browser. They are prototypes: small, quick, and open to change.

**Melt Down** (after Michael Winter). A metaphor about running a nuclear plant on the worst day of its life. Nine meters, sixteen disasters, seven commands, and every command helps one thing and hurts another. Bring everything back to 5 before the core reaches 9.

**Shooting Gallery** (after Frank B. Rowlett, Jr.). A metaphor at the carnival: twenty shots at rows of stars, clowns, pipes and ducks, and now and then an arrow worth a hundred.

**Cryptogram** (after Rik Pierce). A metaphor in letter substitution: crack a saying, one letter at a time, or type yer own for a friend.

**Morse Code Tutor** (after Donald E. Smith). The tutor, made into a metaphor: copy characters by ear, two new ones each level, the code gets faster, three lives, and streaks multiply the score. A sending keyer and a buddy mode are there too.

**Storybook** (after Otto Hansen). A toy, not a metaphor: answer ten questions and it tells a picture story starring the child reading it.

### How to play

- Open the hub and pick one. Each runs on keyboard, mouse or touch.
- Best scores and times are kept in yer browser.
- Found a bug that is not a metaphor? Leave a comment.

### About it

The original listings are in *SoftSide* Vol. 1 No. 11 (August 1979). These are not ports: the rules and the feel were taken from the listings and rebuilt in Odin compiled to WebAssembly.
