## Page text

**Latest version:** https://playdeezgames.github.io/prototypes-of-splorr/

*SoftSide* magazine printed BASIC programs for its readers to type in by hand. These are new takes on thirty of them from seven issues, rebuilt for the browser and gathered on one hub you can sort and search. They are prototypes: small, quick, and open to change.

### From October 1978 (the first issue)

**Pillbox** (after Gene Perkins). A metaphor in artillery: two pillboxes, one mountain, a barrel angle and a muzzle velocity. First to five hits wins the war. Play the computer or a friend.

**Gunnery Mate** (after "Death Star"). A metaphor at the gun deck: raiders drift over yer batteries, and you fire when one is dead centre. Twenty-one shots, then the Overlord decides yer rank.

**State Capital Quiz**. The quiz, made into a metaphor: fifty capitals, three choices each, a clock, three apples and streaks that multiply the score. A miss comes back later.

**Cribbage**. A metaphor with a peg board: two-handed cribbage against the computer, to 121.

**Calculator**. A tool: a desk calculator with a running total, a paper tape and a memory.

### From November 1978

**Troll's Gold** (after George Blank). A metaphor in a cave of thirty-six rooms whose doors change every turn: take the gold from the deepest room and carry it out while the troll comes for you.

**End Zone** (after Roger Robitaille). A metaphor on the gridiron: four quarters of hot-seat football with secret plays, secret defences, punts, field goals and the clock. Or take on a computer coach.

**Shopping List**. A tool: sixteen aisles to tap through, then a notepad list to tick off in the store. Add yer own items (the cheese, this time).

**Octal to Hex** (after Lance Micklus). A tool: three octal digits to a byte in hex, decimal and binary, bits to flip, and the TRS-80 character it makes.

### From December 1978

**Santa Paravia** (after "Santa Paravia en Fiumaccio" by George Blank). A metaphor of 15th-century Italy: one to six rulers of city-states, grain, rats, taxes, justice, cathedrals and invasions, from Sir or Lady to King or Queen before you die. Any seat can be the computer.

**Spelling Bee** (after Lance Micklus). A metaphor in memory: the word flashes up, then spell it. Shorter looks, longer words, three bees.

**Biorhythms** (after Frank B. Rowlett, Jr.). A toy: yer three curves for any month, critical days marked. Just for fun, as the magazine said.

**Mortgage** (after Russell Starkey). A tool: the payment, a green-bar schedule, totals, a chart, and how much sooner an extra payment ends it.

**Ham Shack Clock** (after Peter Ashley). A tool: local time and GMT, and yer call sign flashed (and keyed in Morse) every ten minutes on the air.

**Chess Clock**. A tool: the classic two hours for forty moves, plus rapid, blitz and bullet with increments. Tap yer own face.

### From January 1979

**'Round the Horn** (after George Blank). A metaphor under sail: one to three clippers race from New York round Cape Horn to San Francisco in 1852, by wind, current and weather. Beat the Flying Cloud's 89 days, if you can.

**Ten Pin Bowling** (after Frank B. Rowlett, Jr.). A metaphor at the lanes: throw straight or break the ball left or right. A straight ball never strikes. One to four bowlers.

**Kiddy Slot** (after David Boike). A toy: ten ZZOOPPS, three reels, a sun, a smiley face and a lollipop.

**Comput-A-Sketch** (after Albert C. Blackwell, Jr.). A toy: draw with the keys, or drag, and shake to clear.

### From February 1979

**Prize Board** (after "Concentration" by Lance Micklus). A metaphor in memory: thirty-two squares hide prizes in pairs. Find a pair to win the prize and a guess at the secret number; guess it to keep yer winnings. Against a friend or a computer contestant.

**Elements Quiz** (after Roger W. Robitaille, Sr.). The quiz, made into a metaphor: all 118 elements by name, symbol and number, against the clock, with three flasks to lose and a periodic table to study.

### From March 1979

**Dive Bomb** (after George Blank). A metaphor at sea: a dive bomber against a ship whose anti-aircraft gun jams after three shots. Dive, level off, drop. Fly either side against the computer, or two at one keyboard.

**Jig Saw** (after David Bohlke). A metaphor in deduction: six pieces hide on a four by four board. The computer names one; find a square it covers, in as few tries as you can. One or two players.

**Tarot** (after Frank B. Rowlett, Jr.). A toy: ask a yes-or-no question and deal the five-card spread. Right side up says yes, upside down no. For fun only.

**Fog Index** (after George Blank). A tool: type or paste yer writing and it counts the sentences, words and long words, and gives the school grade a reader needs to follow it. Or count by hand, the 1979 way.

### From August 1979

**Melt Down** (after Michael Winter). A metaphor about running a nuclear plant on the worst day of its life. Nine meters, sixteen disasters, seven commands, and every command helps one thing and hurts another.

**Shooting Gallery** (after Frank B. Rowlett, Jr.). A metaphor at the carnival: twenty shots at rows of stars, clowns, pipes and ducks, and now and then an arrow worth a hundred.

**Cryptogram** (after Rik Pierce). A metaphor in letter substitution: crack a saying, one letter at a time, or type yer own for a friend.

**Morse Code Tutor** (after Donald E. Smith). The tutor, made into a metaphor: copy characters by ear, with levels, lives and streaks. A sending keyer and a buddy mode are there too.

**Storybook** (after Otto Hansen). A toy: answer ten questions and it tells a picture story starring the child reading it.

### How to play

- Open the hub and pick one; sort or filter by name, type, issue or author. Each runs on keyboard, mouse or touch.
- Best scores, records, lists, sketches and yer Fog Index text are kept in yer browser.
- Found a bug that is not a metaphor? Leave a comment.

### About it

The original listings are in *SoftSide* Vol. 1 No. 1 to No. 6 (October 1978 to March 1979) and Vol. 1 No. 11 (August 1979). These are not ports: the rules and the feel were taken from the listings and rebuilt in Odin compiled to WebAssembly.
